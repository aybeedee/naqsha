# Pilot selection evidence

## Recommendation

Use a roughly 17 km² central Lahore pilot around Lakshmi Chowk, GPO, Cooper
Road, Kashmir Road, and Lawrence Road, subject to the same automated terrain
gate applied to the provisional Gulberg–Liberty test.

This recommendation is driven by validation value rather than an assumption
that central Lahore is the city's most flood-prone area.

The automated terrain gate was subsequently run over this 16.83 km² candidate.
It failed: the three-way top-hotspot Jaccard was 0.039, the median elevation
range across sources was 4.09 m, and 66–69% of derived flow directions changed
by more than 45° in comparisons with the SRTM-family surface. The area remains
preferred for validation evidence, but it is not approved for authoritative
hydraulic depths from global terrain.

## Evidence

A published Lahore field study reports 118 observed ponding locations, 43 with
more than three inches of ponding, and nine named locations reaching up to two
feet. It reports up to three feet at Dyal Singh College/Lakshmi Chowk, ten
inches at Cooper Road, eight inches at Kashmir Road, and a mapped depression at
the GPO bus stop. These provide validation targets with observed depths rather
than only news descriptions.

Source: [Rainwater Harvesting Potential and Utilization for Artificial
Recharge of Groundwater Using Stormwater in Lahore,
Pakistan](https://psecommunity.org/wp-content/plugins/wpor/includes/file/1911/LAPSE-2019.1212-1v1.pdf)

The Punjab Municipal Development Fund reports that WASA identified 22 sore
points and built storage tanks at Lawrence Road and Kashmir Road, among other
locations. It records roughly 18 inches of ponding near the Lahore Zoo entrance
before the Lawrence Road intervention. That creates a rare retrospective test:
can the model represent baseline ponding and the direction of benefit from
added storage?

Source: [Study on Rainwater Storage Facilities at Various Cities of
Punjab](https://pmdfc.punjab.gov.pk/system/files/Study%20on%20Rainwater-Final.pdf)

WASA publishes annual monsoon rain records, and PMD flood bulletins expose
station totals including Lakshmi Chowk, Gulberg, Jail Road, and other Lahore
stations. These can constrain event totals even where sub-hourly hyetographs
remain unavailable.

Sources: [WASA rain records](https://wasa.punjab.gov.pk/node/107), [PMD Flood
Forecasting Division bulletins](https://ffd.pmd.gov.pk/bulletin/)

## Candidate comparison

| Candidate | Historical depth evidence | Rainfall observations | Intervention test | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Central: Lakshmi–GPO–Lawrence | Strong | Strong | Storage tanks and drainage works | Preferred |
| Gulberg–Liberty | Moderate | Strong | Recharge wells | Retain as alternative |
| Johar Town–Canal Road | Event reports | Moderate | No public asset detail found yet | Secondary |
| DHA Phase 6–Airport | Strong 2024 event reports | Airport observations | Drainage detail unavailable | Secondary |

NDMA's 2024 review independently lists Model Town, Johar Town, DHA Phase 6,
Shahdara, Tajpura Bazar, Canal Road, and Services Hospital among affected
locations. These remain useful out-of-pilot validation points and future
candidates. Source: [NDMA analytical review of 2024 urban
flooding](https://www.ndma.gov.pk/storage/publications/December2024/OJVEu8JCwHDSN32K3Han.pdf)

## Remaining limitations

- Published depths are sparse observations, not continuous inundation maps.
- Some observations predate drainage/storage works and must be paired with the
  correct infrastructure state.
- News imagery must be timestamped and geolocated before use as validation.
- A final boundary must include the contributing surface catchment, not merely
  the named ponding points; the rectangle is an initial data-audit extent.
